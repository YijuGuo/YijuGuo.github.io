## Motivation

Through comparing token-level gradient differences between LLaMA-70B and LLaMA-1B, we found that **small model reasoning failures often stem from over-attending to distracting tokens while ignoring critical ones**. Simply removing these distracting tokens improves accuracy by 20% on math tasks and 10% on code tasks.

## Method

We propose **Learning to Focus (LeaF)**, a two-stage causal distillation framework:

### Stage 1: Confounding Token Detection

LeaF identifies confounding tokens via teacher-student **gradient-based comparisons** on sensitivity discrepancies, effectively pruning interfering tokens from training corpora and generating high-quality **counterfactual samples** through span pruning.

### Stage 2: Causal Attention Distillation

By contrasting model outputs on original and counterfactual samples through a **hybrid distillation loss** (standard KL divergence + counterfactual KL divergence), LeaF guides the student model to learn genuine causal dependencies.

## Results

Compared to standard distillation:

- **Math reasoning** (GSM8K, MATH, OlympiadBench): **+2.41%**
- **Code generation** (HumanEval+, LeetCode, LiveCodeBench): **+2.48%**
- **Multi-hop reasoning** (2WikiMQA, Musique, HotpotQA): **+3.24%**
